# Metagenomics-analysis-Iron-rich-Zetaproteobacteria-mats-in-the-low-temperature-MIR-zone-of-the-MAR
Numerous studies have focused on the iron-rich microbial mats of the Lō‘ihi seamount (volcanic influence), but very few studies have documented the Arctic and Atlantic oceans, where microbial mats have formed as a result of intense hydrothermal activity. (Wiersen et al., 1993; Scott et al., 2015; Vander-Roost, 2017, 2018; Mc Allister et al., 2019; Hribovšek et al., 2023; Astroch-Cardona et al., 2023, 2024a). Given the limited information available on these mats that formed on old massive sulfide mounds at the MAR, the study of new low-temperature hydrothermal zones offers unprecedented opportunities to deepen our understanding of community diversity and to better characterize their functioning. Therefore, the objective of this study is to characterize the microbial communities of the unexplored low-temperature MIR (LT-MIR) zone, and to compare this diversity and potential functioning across different hydrothermal fields (Arctic and Atlantic oceans). 
The MIR mound is a LT zone showing several diffuse fluid emissions, associated with orange – yellow fluffy (veil) microbial mats and located at the east of the TAG hydrothermal vent field (Active Mound) (Peletter et al., 2024). The LT-MIR microbial communities were analyzed combining metabarcoding, metagenomics, and imaging approaches. Due to the abundance of Zetaproteobacteria in these fluffy veil orange-yellowish mats, their taxonomic diversity, functional potential, and morphologies of their iron (oxy)hydroxides fibrils were examined in more details.
